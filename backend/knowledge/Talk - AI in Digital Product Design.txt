Page 7: FRAMING · 04
Framing in Design
Vermaas, Dorst & Thurgood, 2015
Transportation
In a music festival, people would be able to get there but also leave when
they want. In the entertainment district, train services ended around the
same time that peak influx of patronage begins. Apart from the obvious
improvement of providing more trains at the nearest station, the designers
also implemented a back-up system of temporary signage to lead towards a
different nearby station that has trains running all night
Three framing steps
S = original situation · G = goal · Ğ = reformulated goal · F[T] = frame
· F[Ť] = new frame
Goal reformulation in a design task
(S,G) → (S,Ğ) or (S,G,F[T]) → (S,Ğ,F[T])
Framing the situation in a design task
(S,G) → (S,G,F[T])
Reframing the situation in a design task
(S,G,F[T]) → (S,G,F[Ť])

Page 8: FRAMING · 05
Case: “Customers Are Cancelling”
Homesite Insurance servicing platform · mcv.design/reducing-policy-cancellations
STATED PROBLEM
“Cancellations are spiking — we have a retention
problem.”
Proposed answer: discounts, outreach, save offers at the point of exit.
Thousands of calls a day at $18 a call.
THE REAL PROBLEM
Customers weren't leaving — they were lost.
Most were moving, buying a first home, or needed temporary cover.
With no transfer or re-quote path in the UI, cancellation was the only
door — and no intent data was captured.
THE REFRAME
From cancellation handling → intent routing
Surface intent early
A moving path in servicing nav, before the cancel flow
Support intent late
Reason-based branching with relevant alternatives
Capture structured data
A reason code on every exit, so the system can learn
$4.3M
premium retained
7,560
customers kept
360K+
calls redirected

Page 9: FRAMING · 06
Case: “Nobody Covers Us”
Veracity Insurance Solutions · virgo-pr.com/veracity-insurance-pr-case-study
STATED PROBLEM
“How do we get journalists to write about our
insurance?”
FLIP, Veracity's food-business insurance brand, had almost no earned
media despite a large market.
THE REAL PROBLEM
The product was never the story.
Reporters won't keep covering policies and coverage limits — but they
will cover the small businesses behind them, which is exactly the data
Veracity already held.
THE REFRAME
“What does Veracity know that journalists want to
know?”
Publish original research
A ~50-page State of the Food Industry report, not a press
release
Split it into many stories
One finding per beat: restaurants, small business, finance,
local
Make it repeatable
The same engine rerun for each brand's community
30+
first placements
15+
research reports
25+
exec bylines
